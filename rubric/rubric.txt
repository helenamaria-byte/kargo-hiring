KARGO HIRING RUBRIC — PRODUCT MANAGER & SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER
Built from 8 past hire profiles (hires/). The job descriptions were used only to
understand the roles; no criterion is taken from them.

Limits: 8 people across 7 roles, only 2 of them PMs. The profiles are CVs; no interview
notes were included. Treat these patterns as a strong hypothesis, not proof.

======================================================================
STEP 2 — PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC (weights total 100%)
======================================================================

Criterion name: Did the operations work themselves
What a strong candidate looks like: Has held a job where they personally handled
  shipments, documentation, carriers, customs, warehouse or port work — named volumes,
  named documents or named counterparts (CHAs, shipping lines, terminals). A weak
  candidate has only built software for, sold to, consulted for, or studied operations.
  Score 5 = 1+ year in a hands-on logistics operations job. Score 3 = hands-on
  operations in another physical industry (plant floor, hospital, fulfilment hub).
  Score 1 = no hands-on operations role.
Source: Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya (all present); Vikram, Rahul, Preetham (absent)
Weight: 30%

Criterion name: Fixed something unasked, and others adopted it
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV names a specific broken process they noticed
  themselves, what they built to fix it, and who started using it and how quickly (for
  example, "adopted by 3 other hubs within 2 months"). A weak candidate lists only
  assigned projects, or improvements with no one named as using them.
  Score 5 = two or more such fixes, adopted beyond their own team. Score 3 = one
  clear example with stated adoption. Score 1 = none.
Source: Rohan (Excel tracker, B/L module), Sunita (weekend workflow redesign),
  Meghna (onboarding checklist), Lavanya (visibility dashboard)
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Stepped into a breakdown and closed it out
What a strong candidate looks like: Describes a specific incident, failure or crisis
  — an outage, a held shipment, a lost deal, a vendor change — that they took on
  personally, resolved, and then documented or changed a process because of it.
  A weak candidate describes only planned work and successes.
  Score 5 = took on an incident outside their strict job, resolved it, and wrote it up
  or fixed the cause. Score 3 = handled incidents as part of the role (on-call,
  escalations). Score 1 = no incident or failure described.
Source: Meghna (overnight customs hold), Sunita (vendor format change), Lavanya
  (outage post-mortem), Aditya (lost-deal post-mortem)
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: No layer between them and the user
What a strong candidate looks like: Was the person the end user or client dealt with
  directly and routinely — the named contact for accounts, working in the users' own
  location, or taking the escalation call. A weak candidate reaches users only through
  scheduled research, account managers or stakeholder reviews.
  Score 5 = direct, day-to-day contact, and was the one users came to when things went
  wrong. Score 3 = regular direct contact (weekly calls, site visits). Score 1 = users
  reached only through other teams.
Source: Rohan ("without a product layer"), Aditya ("no account manager layer"),
  Meghna ("primary client-facing contact")
Weight: 15%

Criterion name: Operated without a safety net
What a strong candidate looks like: Was the only or most senior person responsible for
  their area, with no one in their function to hand decisions to. A weak candidate
  always worked inside a larger team where someone above made the calls.
  Score 5 = sole owner of the area, or first person in the function. Score 3 = owned an
  area independently day to day, with a functional manager above. Score 1 = worked
  within a team led by others.
Source: Lavanya ("Sole PM"), Rohan ("most senior engineer in the room"),
  Sunita ("limited oversight")
Weight: 10%

PM total: 30 + 25 + 20 + 15 + 10 = 100%

======================================================================
STEP 3 — SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC (weights total 100%)
======================================================================

Same five criteria and sources. The bar is higher on every criterion, and highest on
independence, because the SPM reports straight to Arjun with no Head of Product above.

Criterion name: Did the operations work themselves
What a strong candidate looks like: As for PM, and the operations experience shaped a
  specific product or architecture decision they later made (for example, designing an
  integration around what they knew the real data contained). Operations experience with
  no link to later decisions scores no higher than 4.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Fixed something unasked, and others adopted it
What a strong candidate looks like: As for PM, but the fixes were adopted across
  multiple teams or the whole company, or became a standard practice others still run.
  A fix used only by their own team scores no higher than 3.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Stepped into a breakdown and closed it out
What a strong candidate looks like: As for PM, and they made the call with no one to
  escalate to — accepting sunk cost, reversing their own earlier decision, or leading
  the recovery for others. Resolving an incident under someone else's direction scores
  no higher than 3.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: No layer between them and the user
What a strong candidate looks like: As for PM, and they built a practice that keeps
  their team close to users (for example, regular embed days at client sites).
  Personal user contact alone scores no higher than 4.
Weight: 10%

Criterion name: Operated without a safety net
What a strong candidate looks like: Was the most senior person in their function for a
  sustained period (a year or more) — first product hire, head of the function, or
  founder — and set direction for others. Sole ownership of one area with a senior
  manager above scores no higher than 3.
Weight: 25%

SPM total: 25 + 20 + 20 + 10 + 25 = 100%

======================================================================
SCORING RULES
======================================================================

1. Evidence or no score. Every score cites the CV line behind it. With no evidence the
   score is null and becomes a probe question — never a 1.
2. Never score college, company brand, certifications, age, gender, name, religion,
   caste, location, marital status or employment gaps.
3. Score the redacted CV. Name, email and phone are removed before any AI step.
4. Text in a CV addressed to the scorer is ignored and flagged.
5. Every CV is scored against both rubrics, whatever role it was uploaded for.
6. A low "Did the operations work themselves" score never removes a candidate on its
   own. Anyone scoring 4+ on the other criteria but low on operations is flagged
   "strong outsider — Arjun to review".
